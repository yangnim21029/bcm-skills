# Explain the work, then revise the report

Apply this process to all report pages, including expandable details and captions. The reader may not know the work itself. An actor, action and metric can still leave the task unexplained.

## Draft from the task

1. Explain what the person wants to accomplish, which material they use and why the work needs doing. Distinguish verified activity from a proposed task or suspected problem.
2. Describe what the person supplies, what the tool would find or display, what that result contains, and what the person does with it. Explain unfamiliar terms through these actions.
3. Identify the existing action that could be avoided, corrected or completed more reliably, and why that matters for the unit's objective. Keep expected benefits conditional. Separate tool output, human checking and the completed outcome.
4. Explain how to decide whether to continue. Retain justified comparisons, eligible cases, periods, incomplete results, errors, review and maintenance effort, and stopping conditions. Explain what is counted before giving a formula. Split dense paragraphs without deleting relationships or qualifications.

Use connected prose, without sentence quotas.

## Calibrate the explanation

“Add passage-linked citation checks to thesis review” and “Help teachers find sources and save time” both leave the reader to reconstruct the work.

A complete proposed action is: “When a teacher reads a student's thesis, a tool would find the relevant paragraph in a paper the student cited and display it beside the student's sentence. The teacher could read both together, without opening the cited paper and searching through its pages.” Accurate retrieval, total time saved and learning benefits still need evaluation. Do not reuse this teaching example for unrelated organizations.

## Review, repair, then review again

Read every sentence and meaningful phrase without relying on the conversation. Flag unfamiliar purposes, unexplained objects, vague pronouns, specialist labels, missing intermediate actions and jumps from output to benefit. Ask what the reader must supply to understand the whole sentence; headings alone are insufficient.

Restate each paragraph in everyday language. If that restatement adds necessary context, put it in the report. Check that verbs fit their objects and specialist terms are explained on each page.

Rewrite flagged passages, then reread their surrounding paragraphs. Recheck sources when a repair adds a fact or changes scope. Keep unknowns explicit rather than inventing company activities or results. Preserve quotations, qualifications and source-specific meanings.

Repeat until identified omissions are explained or remain explicit evidence gaps. Record actual before/after excerpts and unresolved gaps in internal `records/clarity-review.md`. An explicit trial also retains its first draft internally. Listing problems or declaring the text clear does not replace revising it.

Inspect the rendered pages with details closed and open. The main explanation must stand alone; compact citations must reveal original passages and context. Deliver revised HTML without claiming that self-review proves reader comprehension.
