# Assess options without turning unknowns into scores

Start with an objective, capability IDs and evidence, and check why this scope was selected. A user-specified local goal can be assessed locally; a company-level question needs a defensible reason for focusing on one business. Detailed documentation, available data and a simple pilot are not evidence that this business deserves priority.

Use the [structured intake](interactive-intake.md) for a new assessment, or reuse its existing record. Ask what decision the user needs to make and what evidence they can directly provide before using external sources to fill material gaps. Explicitly skipped questions remain provisional or unknown; an available product page cannot answer them on the user's behalf.

Keep the assessment provisional and look for actual counterexamples to the proposed gap, causal effect and priority. Existing automation may contradict the claimed gap without establishing that no further improvement is possible. Evidence of a different limiting condition can redirect the investigation. Do not turn these possibilities into invented company stories, or force a contrarian conclusion when the evidence does not support one.

Gather additional information to distinguish the competing explanations, rather than immediately filling every unknown or collecting more confirmations. Change the scope, candidate or confidence when warranted. If the evidence cannot establish which goal matters most, present conditional options and the observation that would distinguish them. Compare the actual bottleneck, proposed change and relevant alternatives. A model producing an alert does not establish that the workflow can make a decision, execute it and verify completion.

Use that reasoning to write the final judgment about the company. The report explains the existing work, proposed improvement, causal mechanism and validation conditions. Keep the history of revising hypotheses or choosing diagrams in the research record. In Chinese, apply the included writing and translation rules to replace abstract terms with concrete actions while preserving evidence limits.

Check data sources, identifiers/time linkage, outcomes needed for the chosen method, access, production updates, response time and owners. Separate data readiness from engineering/integration, governance, people and organization. Assign proposed responsibilities explicitly for ongoing data quality, operational exceptions, outcome review and changes.

Opportunity positioning asks where the portfolio concentrates: front/back office, products/services or core capabilities, and everyday improvement or competitive change. Value/feasibility asks how valuable and implementable an option is for the chosen enterprise. Do not call the matrix an opportunity radar or infer ROI from a radial position.

Use numbers only with a defined scale and a basis. Public company performance is not an internal readiness score. Keep unscored options visible, state what would resolve them, and sequence prerequisites before dependent applications. A high-value/high-feasibility point still needs authority, resources and validation before deployment.

Seven readiness dimensions are strategy, value, organization, people/culture, governance, engineering and data. Compare current conditions with a justified target for the task. Null means unknown, never zero. Gaps show work to discuss, not a universal maturity ranking or an order to aim for 5 everywhere.

Evaluate perception, decision, action, autonomy, adaptation and knowledge separately. System functions, authorization and accuracy are different questions. Describe veto windows, mandatory notification, notification on inquiry, machine-selected notification or absence of human participation explicitly; if using webinar levels, 5→1 means fewer human-intervention conditions. These numbers are source-specific, not universal standards.

Defend concerns individual-work benefits, Extend team/process change, Upend uncertain new value/markets. None guarantees ROI or requires a portfolio share. Keep business, AI, IT and data plans consistent; run scoped prototypes alongside top-down planning and revisit results on a suitable cadence.

Source basis: Gartner, Alexander Hoeppe, “Accelerate Your AI Journey With Business Capability Maps,” particularly 00:22:56–00:42:55 and 00:54:46–01:00:16. Interpretations are methodological guidance, not official company assessments.
