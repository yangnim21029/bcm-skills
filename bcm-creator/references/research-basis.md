# Research basis and limits

Read when justifying the modeling method or resolving an ambiguous assessment. These sources inform the appended checks; they do not replace the seven decision lenses, company evidence or current visual grammar.

## Definitions need an explicit scope

Offerman, Stettina and Plaat (2017), *Business Capabilities: A Systematic Literature Review and a Research Agenda*. [Author-hosted full text](https://liacs.leidenuniv.nl/~plaata1/Offerman2017_SLR.pdf), sections V.B and VI.A–C.

The review identifies disagreement about capability definitions and distinguishes maps, frameworks, process taxonomies and maturity models. Its search was conducted in January 2016; its proposed definition and research agenda are not a validated universal standard.

Application here: define the work and boundary of a capability, then attach resources and processes. Evidence for a component supports that component's existence; broader integration or performance requires separate support.

## Map facts and assess attributes separately

Kotusev and Alwadain (2024; online 2023), *Modeling Business Capabilities in Enterprise Architecture Practice: The Case of Business Capability Models*. [DOI: 10.1080/10580530.2023.2231635](https://doi.org/10.1080/10580530.2023.2231635), pp. 206–217, especially Table 2, p. 215.

[Author-hosted full text](https://kotusev.com/Modeling%20Business%20Capabilities%20in%20Enterprise%20Architecture%20Practice%20-%20The%20Case%20of%20Business%20Capability%20Models.pdf) provides the retrieved copy, including Table 2.

The qualitative study distinguishes mappings of problems, objectives and investments from assessments of maturity, importance and gaps. Its cases support several complementary uses, not one universally superior model or a causal estimate of investment returns.

Application here: keep the capability base stable while selecting an overlay for the decision. Give each color scale one meaning. The skill's maintain/monitor/innovate attention categories are its own decision lens, not a maturity scale validated by this paper.

## Feasibility depends on usable evidence

Khosroshahi et al. (2018), *Business Capability Maps: Current Practices and Use Cases for Enterprise Architecture Management*. [HICSS publication record](https://aisel.aisnet.org/hicss-51/os/enterprise_architecture/2/), sections 4–6, pp. 4607–4611; DOI: 10.24251/HICSS.2018.581.

Interviews with 25 organizations identified 14 use cases. Feasibility assessments included information availability, quality and implementation effort. The reported benefit judgments are practitioner ratings, not measured causal returns, and the study does not supply a complete method for defining capabilities or designing heatmaps.

Application here: connect mapped information to the decision it supports, account for maintaining that information, and separate feasibility from expected contribution. Do not transfer the study's use-case ranking to a company's AI investments.

## Additional safeguards from local review

The component-status check, task denominators, outcome windows, treatment of referrals and bounded comparisons also address ambiguities found in the local library trial. They are practical adaptations, not findings demonstrated by these three studies. Record their assumptions internally and express only the resulting company-specific judgment in the reading page.
