TSMC — Investment Options

2026-11-01

The working objective is to deliver customer orders that meet agreed quality requirements on time. Compare the four proposals against current TSMC systems and simpler process changes. A local team would first need to show where a delay, a quality problem or repeated work occurs; the public sources do not establish which proposal would provide the greatest benefit.

The next decision is which documented task merits a bounded comparison. It requires current performance, an executable improvement and its full operating cost.

1. Bring the records for a process investigation into one view

A process engineer investigating an unusual wafer result needs to find out which production conditions may explain it and which response is allowed. Wafers are silicon discs on which chips are made; they move through production in groups called lots. TSMC already describes intelligent diagnosis and process control. [8] The proposed tool would take one selected investigation and display its measurements, equipment history, process settings, similar past cases and exact passages from the applicable procedures. It would highlight differences between the cases and any missing records so the engineer can check what applies.

The suspected opportunity is time lost assembling or checking evidence. That loss has not been established publicly. The engineer would use the checked records to choose the next investigation or request an approved action, such as temporarily holding the affected wafers for review. Compare the tool with existing engineering tools and a standard set of collected records. Fund a wider trial only if it reduces total review and rework while preserving decision quality and following the case through its final production decision.

Capability: Process & yield control

How to run and check the trial

What to check in current work: Confirm whether evidence retrieval or interpretation still delays a selected class of process investigations, and whether the existing engineering-analysis tools already address that delay.

Which documents and permissions are needed: Use only approved case records, measurements, tool identifiers, versions of process settings and procedures, event times, prior production decisions and later outcomes. Confirm that historical cases can be reconstructed as they appeared at the decision time. Limit retrieval by site, technology and user permission; current identifiers, access and update delays remain unverified.

What the people and the tool would do: The engineer selects an eligible case. The tool retrieves relevant records and displays evidence, differences and uncertainty; it can draft an investigation note. The engineer checks it, requests an authorized response through the existing system and records the reason. The quality reviewer later links that action to the final decision about the affected group of wafers. No model output counts as a completed production decision.

Who would do each job: Proposed responsibilities: a process-engineering owner selects cases and responds to exceptions; a steward of manufacturing data resolves linkage and version defects; a quality reviewer checks decisions and outcomes; a software owner maintains retrieval and rollback. Their identities, time and recurring cost are unknown.

Who can make decisions: Read-only evidence retrieval and draft notes are the proposed starting permissions. Temporarily stopping affected wafers, releasing a wafer group and changing process settings require the existing authorized owner. Missing or conflicting evidence triggers referral; silence never approves an action.

How to test whether the change helps: Compare the tool, the current workflow and a standard collection of production records on the same defined type of investigation, using only information available when each decision was made. Agree in advance the dates for accepting cases and the period for following their outcomes. Include every eligible new case within those dates and report cases still open at the end separately. Count correct evidence use, unsafe or unsupported recommendations, total engineer and reviewer minutes, repeated investigations and later reversals. Reconstructing past cases can test decision support; a later authorized trial must establish whether production outcomes improve. Stop on a critical unsupported action or access breach. Advance only if decision quality remains acceptable and the saved effort covers the agreed operating cost.

What to compare with: Existing diagnosis and knowledge-search tools; a standard collection of production records and applicable procedure versions; better escalation routing. Prefer those if evidence organization, rather than inference, explains the loss.

2. Check which measurements could change a quality decision

Inspection shows defects; metrology measures properties of a wafer, the silicon disc on which chips are made, or of the production process. TSMC already applies AI in both areas, including estimating measurements from other production data. [1] A proposed extension would examine a defined group of wafers and show which additional approved measurement could resolve an uncertain quality decision. The display would include the suspected issue, supporting observations, uncertainty and the cases for which the model has little relevant experience.

A quality or process engineer would decide whether to request the measurement and then check whether its result changed the decision to hold, release or further investigate the wafers. The potential benefit is less unproductive measurement or earlier detection of a consequential defect. It requires evidence that current sampling leaves a material problem; a more accurate prediction alone does not establish that fewer measurements are safe.

Capability: Defect & measurement management

How to run and check the trial

What to check in current work: Identify an inspection or measurement task with a demonstrated trade-off between missed consequential defects, review effort and measurement delay. The public record establishes existing methods, not the remaining error distribution.

Which documents and permissions are needed: Link wafer and wafer-group identifiers, process step, tool and version of process settings, inspection images or signals, physical measurements and independently reviewed defects and production decisions. Record which wafers were sampled and why. Separate product, site and ramp period. Confirm permission to use the data and response deadlines before selecting a method.

What the people and the tool would do: The tool ranks candidate measurements within an already permitted set and explains the evidence behind each suggestion. The quality/process engineer reviews the list and records the actual measurement request. The measurement result and final production decision return to the evaluation record. Cases outside the supported population remain on the established inspection route.

Who would do each job: Proposed responsibilities: quality owns acceptable error and sampling criteria; process/metrology engineers act on suggestions; a data steward maintains record links and sampling history; the model owner monitors changing inputs and performance. Confirm review cover, equipment availability and maintenance effort.

Who can make decisions: Begin with retrospective evaluation and then suggestions shown to reviewers while the approved sampling plan continues unchanged. Any live change to inspection coverage or release of a wafer group requires a separately approved quality procedure. The model cannot waive a required test.

How to test whether the change helps: Agree which wafers and dates the comparison covers, including normal variation and relevant known defects. Preserve the original time order so later information cannot help an earlier decision. Compare the proposed system with the current inspection and sampling policy and a revised rule-based policy. Count missed consequential defects, unnecessary measurements, decision delay, review minutes and final production decisions for every included wafer. Where permitted, independently inspect a sample of wafers the proposal would not select; an unmeasured wafer cannot be assumed clean. Report cases without an independently checked result and differences across products, sites and ramp periods. Stop if consequential misses exceed the bound agreed before the trial or if reviewers cannot independently check the proposed choice.

What to compare with: Revise sampling rules, inspect a targeted additional population, recalibrate equipment or clarify how defects are classified. These may solve the same problem with lower review and maintenance cost.

3. Explain feasible responses to a wafer or packaging capacity exception

A capacity planner must determine whether a customer requirement can be met through the approved wafer-production steps and packaging operations. TSMC already uses AI dispatching to help decide which waiting production work goes next, and 3DFabric offers several ways to assemble chips into packages. [7][6] The proposed tool would take one planning exception, such as a requirement that the current plan cannot satisfy, and compare available responses. Each scenario would show an available production slot or already approved packaging route, its limiting resource, expected timing, affected commitments and assumptions requiring confirmation.

The planner would review the scenario with wafer and packaging teams before the authorized commercial owner changes a promise. The test is whether this makes a feasible decision faster or avoids an overlooked conflict. If no route has enough capacity and the required qualification, the useful result is an explanation of the constraint and referral to the responsible team. Additional capacity or a negotiated date may then be the business response.

Capability: Capacity commitment

How to run and check the trial

What to check in current work: Confirm whether exception decisions are delayed by assembling constraints or comparing alternatives, rather than by a known shortage of qualified physical capacity. Retain demand uncertainty and customer changes as separate causes.

Which documents and permissions are needed: Use versioned customer requirements, existing commitments, work-in-process status, eligible route/tool constraints, packaging availability, material readiness and approved transfer qualifications. Store the time each input became available. Reconcile units and planning horizons across wafer and packaging operations. Public portals do not establish permission to access these internal planning feeds.

What the people and the tool would do: The tool detects conflicts in a proposed plan and produces scenarios through a validated constraint checker or scheduling engine. Any language model explains the scenario from those results. Manufacturing teams confirm assumptions, the planner recommends a response, and the authorized owner records the commitment. Later tracking distinguishes an accepted plan from a delivery actually completed.

Who would do each job: Proposed responsibilities: the capacity-planning owner sets the decision scope; wafer and packaging planners validate constraints; customer-facing staff confirm commitments; a planning-data owner maintains input definitions and freshness. Ownership across partner interfaces and review time remain unknown.

Who can make decisions: Initially, show planning suggestions to reviewers while the existing planning process controls all decisions. The system cannot reprioritize customer orders, dispatch production, change a route or promise a date. Existing planning and commercial authority approve each change. Conflicts with existing commitments must be visible before approval.

How to test whether the change helps: Replay all planning exceptions meeting the agreed criteria during a fixed period using only information available then. Compare current planning, a constraint checklist and the proposed system on feasible plans found, violated constraints and planner/reviewer time. Follow any later authorized pilot through the original commitment window and record fulfilled, late, revised and cancelled commitments separately. Count justified infeasibility as correct handling, not successful delivery. Do not attribute improvement to the model if added tools, capacity or a changed demand mix explain it. Stop when constraint violations or verification effort outweigh the benefit.

What to compare with: Improve the shared constraint checklist and planning meeting; correct stale inputs; add qualified capacity or partner support; renegotiate timing. A new prediction model is unnecessary when the same response follows from an already known hard constraint.

Sources: [6] [7]

4. Answer design questions with the applicable rule and version

A design-support specialist helps a customer determine which technology rule or design asset applies before a design is submitted for manufacturing. TSMC’s Open Innovation Platform supplies design assets and tools, while eFoundry provides technical information exchange. [3][5] The proposed tool would take the question, the customer’s permitted technology context and the relevant document version. It would display the supporting rule beside the draft answer, identify exceptions and show conflicts between versions.

The specialist would verify the passages, request missing context and return the checked response through the established customer channel. The potential benefit is less repeated searching and fewer answers based on the wrong version. Compare it with current search and a maintained question index. Faster drafting is useful only if the customer’s issue is correctly resolved without added checking or later design rework.

Capability: Design enablement

How to run and check the trial

What to check in current work: Confirm a recurring class of design questions that consumes material specialist or customer time after current documentation and search are used. No source establishes that customers lack access to design guidance.

Which documents and permissions are needed: Use approved technology documents, design-rule versions, customer access permissions, question context, checked historical answers and case closure or reopening records. Keep customer-specific material partitioned. Define document update/withdrawal handling and who can correct a disputed answer; none of these trial interfaces is confirmed.

What the people and the tool would do: The specialist selects the permitted technology context and submits the question. The tool retrieves applicable passages and drafts an answer with exceptions and missing inputs. The specialist verifies or rejects it, sends the approved answer and checks resolution. A correct referral is recorded as handling, while the customer issue remains open until resolved.

Who would do each job: Proposed responsibilities: a design-enablement lead owns answer quality and exceptions; a document owner controls versions; a security owner reviews access permissions; support staff verify replies; a software owner maintains retrieval. Confirm expert availability and total customer/engineer effort.

Who can make decisions: Read permitted documents and draft internal replies only. The tool cannot grant a rule waiver, certify a design or send a customer response. The designated specialist approves every answer; unresolvable version or access-permission conflicts are referred.

How to test whether the change helps: Compare current search, a maintained question index and the proposed display on all permitted questions meeting agreed criteria within one technology and document-version scope. Set the dates for accepting questions and how long to follow each answer before the trial. Count correct resolutions, answers based on the wrong version, referrals, reopened questions and total customer and specialist minutes; retain abandoned or unresolved cases. Stop on cross-customer disclosure or an uncorrected critical rule error. Advance only if resolution quality is maintained and the complete task saves enough effort to cover upkeep.

What to compare with: Improve document navigation, version notices, standard answers and specialist routing. Choose the simpler approach if questions repeat predictably or document defects account for most rework.

Sources: [3] [5]

The option order follows the work being compared. It does not rank returns or approve deployment. These proposals concentrate on existing foundry work; no evidence here justifies a new-market bet. Before any trial, define its site, technology, customer permissions, case population and period.
