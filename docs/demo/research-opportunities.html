Research opportunities · 1 November 2026

Research to test against TSMC’s existing work

TSMC already reports AI for yield analysis, process control, equipment failure detection, virtual metrology (estimating wafer properties from production readings) and wafer inspection; it also describes introducing generative AI for engineering analysis. [1] [1] Its public operations description includes AI dispatch, which chooses the next waiting group of wafers for a machine. [7] The papers below offer possible comparisons for those capabilities. A local case must show what the existing system leaves unresolved before a new method deserves a trial.

The most useful next reading concerns evidence for process investigations and realistic dispatch evaluation. Measurement uncertainty provides design background. None of these papers establishes TSMC’s internal losses, readiness, return on investment or a safe reduction in inspection. The proposed business outcome remains dependable customer supply; a ranked cause, a draft report or a simulated schedule is an intermediate result.

Cross-Process Defect Attribution using Potential Loss Analysis

Idé, Tsuyoshi, Miyaguchi, Kohei
arXiv 2508.00895v1
2025-07-27

After an inspection finds more defects than expected, a process engineer needs to trace where they arose. The engineer would supply wafer routes, equipment and recipe identifiers (a recipe specifies processing settings), timestamps and defects per unit area (defect density). The method would score earlier steps, helping the engineer choose which records to investigate before approving a correction.

At Albany NanoTech, the study used histories for 787 wafers. It reports prediction correlations of 0.87 for the proposed model and 0.61 for the linear comparison; attribution examples point to long tool waits. Prediction accuracy and suspected contributors do not establish a corrective action’s effect. [12] [12]

Read: Selected sections: §§3.1–3.2, 4.4–4.5, 5–6; introductory context. Mathematical derivation not independently checked.

Conditional offline comparison

Compare against TSMC’s existing engineering analysis and a simple route/recipe cohort comparison on later, held-out cases. Reject if engineers cannot confirm more useful leads after counting checking time and false leads. A completed result is a verified correction with subsequent defect measurements, not a ranked list.

Scalability of Reinforcement Learning Methods for Dispatching in Semiconductor Frontend Fabs: A Comparison of Open-Source Models with Real Industry Datasets

Stöckermann, Patrick, Südfeld, Henning, Immordino, Alessandro et al.
arXiv 2505.11135v1
2025-05-16

A production planner deciding which waiting lot (group of wafers) a machine should process next would supply due dates, processing and setup times, eligible machines, and rules for combining lots. A learned policy would rank the lots; a simulator would show consequences before the planner accepts a change. [13]

The industry-derived simulation reports up to 4% less tardiness (delay beyond due dates) and 1% more throughput (completed wafers per period). Unseen scenarios sometimes worsen individual metrics. The commercial industry dataset is unavailable. These are simulation results, with expert-tuned dispatch rules as the demanding comparison. [13] [13]

Read: Selected sections: §2.2, §3, §4.1 and §§4.3–4.5, §§5–6, data availability; figures read through captions, not independently digitized.

Read first for evaluation design

Compare with TSMC’s current AI dispatch and tuned rules under identical demand and failure scenarios. Stop if lateness improves by sacrificing completed wafers or constraint compliance. Requires a validated local simulator, planner time and training compute. Front-end dispatch evidence leaves wafer-to-package coordination untested.

Measurement Uncertainty: Relating the uncertainties of physical and virtual measurements

Cramer, Simon, Müller, Tobias, Schmitt, Robert H.
arXiv 2402.13666v1
2024-02-21

A quality engineer deciding which wafers need physical measurement would supply process readings linked to measured quality properties. A probabilistic model would display a predicted value and its uncertainty, so the engineer can identify cases needing physical measurement. The proposed outcome is fewer unnecessary measurements without more missed out-of-limit wafers.

The paper relates physical and predicted measurement uncertainty; it does not provide a TSMC sampling result. It warns that tool servicing can invalidate a model, and leaves boundaries for probabilistic uncertainty methods unresolved. [14] [14]

Read: Selected sections: §3 definitions and documentation, §§4–5. Appendices and all derivations not independently checked.

Background for measurement controls

Use as design background before reducing any measurements. Compare with the current sampling rule and existing virtual metrology on held-out time periods, including tool changes. Stop if the estimated uncertainty fails to cover actual errors or misses out-of-limit cases. Prediction on every wafer does not mean every defect was detected.

SemiFA: An Agentic Multi-Modal Framework for Autonomous Semiconductor Failure Analysis Report Generation

Kaushik, Shivam Chand
arXiv 2604.13236v1
2026-04-14

An engineer documenting findings after a failed inspection could supply wafer maps (showing where defects appear), equipment messages and previous cases. The system would draft defect descriptions, cause hypotheses and suggested actions. A possible TSMC use would display supporting records beside each draft statement for verification before the engineer decides the case.

Training narratives are generated by a language model. The root-cause ablation uses five cases, authored telemetry and history, and a GPT-4o judge; removing retrieval scores 3.73 versus 3.60 for the full system. Performance on scanning electron microscope or optical images remains untested in the reported evaluation. [15] [15] [15]

Read: Selected sections: III, IV-A, V-A–V-C, VI–VII, code/data statement. Repository, licenses and runtime not verified.

Conditional report-assistance reading; reject autonomous correction

Compare evidence retrieval plus a report template with the multi-agent design. Reject automatic changes to machine or process settings from this evidence. Before a local trial, measure unsupported statements and total engineer review time on resolved cases; a fast draft is not a verified diagnosis or recovered yield.

Tassel, Pierre, Kovács, Benjamin, Gebser, Martin et al.
arXiv 2302.07162v1
2023-02-14

A candidate scheduling study for choosing the next production operation. A local evaluation would require current lot queues, tool availability and a validated simulator, then show planners candidate sequences and their predicted completion consequences.

The abstract reports self-supervised and reinforcement learning outperforming hierarchical dispatch strategies in semiconductor manufacturing models. The abstract was read; numerical comparisons and constraints were not checked. [16]

Read: Metadata and abstract only; methods, results and limitations not assessed.

Background; full text needed

Read alongside the later scalability paper. No trial recommendation from this abstract. Establish which current dispatch rules it beats and whether the simulation includes TSMC’s relevant constraints before spending integration effort.

SePArate: Segmenting Patterns from Defects in Wafer Manufacturing Using Weak Supervision

Kwon, Dain, Shin, Changmin, Park, Sunjong et al.
arXiv 2608.30410v1
2026-08-31

An inspection engineer could provide wafer maps with whole-image defect labels. The proposed method separates spatial patterns into pixels, which could help an engineer inspect overlapping patterns before investigating their causes.

The abstract describes encoder pretraining, transfer of spatial information and training with synthetic mixed defects, using image-level labels. It claims better segmentation than comparisons, without enough detail here to assess operational missed defects. [17]

Read: Metadata and abstract only; methods, results and limitations not assessed.

Worth reading if pattern separation is a measured bottleneck

First read the full evaluation and compare with TSMC’s deployed inspection and existing pattern rules. This is a reading lead, not approval to change sampling. Continue only if pattern separation improves reviewed decisions on current product and process conditions.

Unsupervised Anomaly Prediction with N-BEATS and Graph Neural Network in Multi-variate Semiconductor Process Time Series

Sorensen, Daniel, Dey, Bappaditya, Hwang, Minjin et al.
arXiv 2510.20718v1
2025-10-23

An equipment engineer could supply aligned sensor time series. The method forecasts readings and flags deviations from expected behavior; the engineer would inspect the affected tool and determine whether a maintenance or process action is warranted.

The abstract compares independent N-BEATS forecasts with a graph model that represents relationships between sensors. It reports forecast horizons in time points, which do not establish how much actionable warning a TSMC engineer would receive. [18]

Read: Metadata and abstract only; methods, results and limitations not assessed.

Worth reading for earlier equipment investigation

Read the full paper before testing. Compare with current alarms and a simple forecasting/residual threshold. Count warning time before confirmed faults, false alerts and engineering effort. An unusual signal alone does not establish a fault or its cause.

Scalable Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning for Factory-wide Dynamic Scheduling

Jang, Jaeyeon, Klabjan, Diego, Liu, Han et al.
arXiv 2409.13571v1
2024-09-20

The proposed approach divides factory scheduling among cooperating agents and adds a rule-based conversion step. A planner could examine coordinated candidate schedules and constraint failures before changing execution.

The abstract reports a leader–follower design, experiments against deep-learning schedulers and a rule-based protection against capacity loss. Dataset fit, tested constraints and numerical results have not been read. [19]

Read: Metadata and abstract only; methods, results and limitations not assessed.

Background; coordination fit not yet assessed

The existing TSMC dispatch system and a constraint-based scenario planner are the relevant local comparisons. Read the full evaluation only if a documented coordination problem persists; factory-wide language does not establish wafer-to-package coverage.

Measure This, Not That: Optimizing the Cost and Model-Based Information Content of Measurements

Wang, Jialu, Peng, Zedong, Hughes, Ryan et al.
arXiv 2406.09557v1
2024-06-13

A research engineer choosing sensors or experiment measurements could supply a physical model, candidate measurements and costs. The optimization would show measurement sets trading cost against information for estimating model parameters.

The abstract’s two cases concern a batch reactor and a rotary packed bed for carbon capture. Its objective concerns parameter estimation, which differs from finding defective wafers during production. [20]

Read: Metadata and abstract only; methods, results and limitations not assessed.

Background outside the immediate wafer-inspection task

Retain as a non-neural optimization alternative for experiment design. Do not use its reported case results to justify reducing production inspection. A TSMC use would first need a suitable process model and a separate definition of acceptable defect risk.

Learning to Branch in Combinatorial Optimization with Graph Pointer Networks

Wang, Rui, Zhou, Zhiming, Zhang, Tao et al.
arXiv 2307.01434v1
2023-07-04

When a planning program searches many possible solutions, it can split the search into smaller cases. This method uses the program’s current state to choose the next variable to split, aiming to find a solution with less computation.

The abstract reports imitation of strong branching and tests on combinatorial optimization benchmarks. This improves a solver component; it is not direct evidence about factory dispatch. [24]

Read: Metadata and abstract only; methods, results and limitations not assessed.

Background only; solver bottleneck unestablished

Only investigate if an existing TSMC optimization solver’s branching time is the measured bottleneck. Compare with the solver’s native rules first. A faster solver has business value only if it yields usable plans within the decision window.

2024-07-08

A software engineer defines available choices, decision context and subsequent feedback. SmartChoices supplies infrastructure to train and evaluate learned choices inside software.

The abstract addresses contextual-bandit decisions such as caching and task scheduling. Its interface separates problem definitions from training and deployment implementation. [25]

Read: Metadata and abstract only, version 3; methods, results and limitations not assessed.

Background outside the immediate operating options

Retain as implementation background. It does not establish semiconductor process constraints or customer-supply improvement. Consider it only after a suitable local decision and feedback signal are defined, and compare with the current heuristic and logging implementation.

What would justify a local comparison

Start with one documented decision that an engineer or planner cannot complete well enough with the current system. Retain the records that were available at that decision time, the recommendation made, the human review and the eventual outcome. Later records must not leak into the model’s inputs.

For a process investigation, count useful confirmed leads, unsupported statements, unresolved cases and total engineer time, then check any approved correction against subsequent measurements. For measurement decisions, count actual out-of-limit wafers missed as well as measurements avoided, with separate checks after tool or process changes. For production planning, include unfinished work, lateness, completed wafers and every relevant constraint; a favorable average can conceal an unacceptable scenario. Compare each method with TSMC’s current workflow and the simpler alternative named in the table.

Connecting wafer dispatch to package production remains a separate hypothesis. It requires package capacity, test and material constraints, actual handoff timing and customer commitments. The front-end scheduling papers above cannot establish that this combined model is valid.
